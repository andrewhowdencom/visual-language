# Principles

## Calm foundation, lively details

Make the working surface quiet enough for the task. Use warm near-white, dark espresso, clear type, and space to establish hierarchy. Put stronger color where it communicates an action, selection, status, meaningful category, or moment of delight. **Do not** turn an approximate neutral-to-accent proportion into a pixel quota. Judge the composition at the scale and context where people will use it.

The character is warm, practical, and slightly reminiscent of late-1970s and early-1980s sporting goods and consumer electronics. Express that through confident typography, useful geometry, tactile surfaces, and restrained motifs. Avoid nostalgia that makes a task harder to read or use.

## One language, native behavior

**Must:** Preserve familiar navigation, inputs, gestures, focus, and system behavior on each platform. Shared color and form do not require identical components. An Android screen may use Material 3 navigation and system bars; a web page should behave like a web page.

**Should:** Use Material 3's semantic color, type, shape, motion, and adaptive-layout ideas as a common vocabulary. Make the product's primary task and content hierarchy more important than the framework's default look.

**May:** Use a flat sun, arc, band, hill, or simple geometric mark as an accent in a large or introductory region. Keep artwork out of task-critical text, charts, inputs, and dense lists.

## Hierarchy and expression

1. Make the next useful action obvious.
2. Group related information with spacing and surface tone before adding borders or more colors.
3. Use one strong focal point per view. A bright detail should earn its place by carrying meaning.
4. Use motion to connect states and locations. Keep it brief, interruptible, and respectful of reduced-motion preferences.
5. Let typography carry structure: large, sturdy headings; compact, readable body text; clear numerals. Avoid novelty fonts in functional UI.

## Material reference

Material Design 3 is the current baseline; Material 3 Expressive expands its palette, typography, shape, motion, and adaptive guidance. Use its system of roles and responsive thinking, then review the result against the actual platform and task. See [Material Design](https://m3.material.io/) and [Material 3 in Compose](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/designsystems/material3).
