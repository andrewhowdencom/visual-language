# Physical space and objects

The office is a real application of this language. Translate the intent—calm foundations, useful hierarchy, lively details—into spatial decisions. A screen color is a reference for mood, not a paint code or material specification.

## Start with use and setting

**Must:** Record the room's dimensions, daylight direction, artificial lighting, acoustic needs, accessibility needs, circulation, existing furniture, budget, and the work done there before fixing a layout or ordering items. Test actual material and paint samples in the room by day and at night. Keep paths, reach, glare, and seating comfort ahead of a visual motif.

## Translate the palette

**Should:** Use warm neutral walls and larger surfaces as a calm background, dark neutral elements for structure and legibility, a selected primary family for a few durable pieces or zones, and vivid accents in replaceable details. Think in relative visual weight rather than fixed percentages. A chair, wall, and small object do not have the same visual footprint or replacement cost.

| Digital role | Possible spatial translation |
| --- | --- |
| Canvas | Walls, ceiling, broad floor or worktop surface |
| Surface | Desk, shelving, table, partition |
| Primary | One furniture family, focal wall, or key wayfinding cue |
| Accent | Upholstery, lamps, art, small storage, signage detail |
| Text / outline | Legible signs, edges, hardware, dark structural rhythm |

## Material, light, and form

**Should:** Pair matte or low-glare finishes with tactile but easy-to-maintain materials. Use clear geometry, generous negative space, and a small number of rounded or arced details. Prefer a few confident shapes over themed decoration everywhere. Choose lighting for the tasks and the human face first; use accent lighting to create depth, not to compensate for poor work light.

## Decide and review

For each proposed item, record function, dimensions, material, color family, durability, maintenance, and how it behaves beside neighboring items. Make a scaled plan and a physical sample board before buying. Review the result from entry, seated, and circulation viewpoints, including at night. The digital [review checklist](review.md) has parallel checks for legibility, hierarchy, and states, but local building and accessibility requirements must be checked separately.
